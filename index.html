City X-ray: Look beneath the headline

How Toronto's public resources are distributed, and why headline totals can mislead.

Six short notes on libraries and budgets show what one big number can leave out.

Scroll to begin

01 · The hook

100 library branches. One in every neighbourhood?

Toronto has 158 neighbourhoods. Many of them have no physical library branch inside their boundaries.

Residents may still use branches in nearby neighbourhoods. This shows location, not travel access.

04 · Per resident

How do library branches compare per resident?

The higher-income group has about 21% more physical branches per 10,000 residents than the lower-income group.

Income groups split Toronto's 158 neighbourhoods into rough thirds by 2020 median household income: lower ≤ CAD 79,000, middle CAD 79,000-90,000, higher > CAD 90,000. Population is the 2021 Census count of people in private households.

The rate does not rise steadily with income; the gap is mainly between the higher group and the other two.

Six branches, all in the lower and middle groups, were closed in October 2026. Counting only open branches, the higher group's rate is about 33% above the lower group's.

05 · Main message

Look beneath the headline

Before judging whether a community is well served, residents should ask what a number includes and how many people it serves.

Headline totals give an incomplete picture of Toronto's public resources.

They cannot show how resources compare across services and communities.

06 · Solution

How can residents make better comparisons?

Specific funding or siting decisions need more evidence; the first step is asking better questions of the data.
